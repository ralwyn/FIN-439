# Scholarship-fund mandate

Fictional client: Campus Opportunity Scholarship Fund, $1 million, $40,000 annual scholarships, at least ten-year horizon, annual rebalancing. The nominal planning target is 6.5% over rolling five-year periods, motivated by 4% spending plus assumed 2.5% inflation. Historical arithmetic means used by the model do not establish that compound target.

Universe: BIL (reserve), IEF (Treasury duration), LQD (investment-grade credit), VT (global equity). At least 10% in BIL, initially $100,000 or 2.5 years of distributions; only BIL counts. Fully invested, long-only; no leverage, shorts, derivatives or individual-stock trades. The minimum-volatility search is on five-percentage-point grid allocations.

Comparison benchmark: 40% LQD / 60% VT, as a transparent investable proxy for the earlier bond/global-equity policy reference. This is a comparison benchmark, not a mandate-compliant recommendation. Additional baselines are equal weight and inverse standalone volatility on the same sample.

Risk measure: annualized sample volatility, not a guarantee about losses. No numerical drawdown tolerance has been established by the fictional client. Pilot is a paper research exercise; no capital is authorized. Liquidity: confirm ETF spreads and ability to fund actual scholarship dates before adoption; listed ETF tradability does not guarantee price stability. Tax status, implementation ownership, maximum acceptable loss and an operational spending policy require committee clarification.

Reference weights for cost comparisons: 60% BIL / 40% VT, the prior Lab 15 starting mix; these are assumed, not actual client holdings. One-way turnover is half the sum of absolute weight changes. Cost drag = turnover × cost in basis points / 10,000, for one annual rebalance. A 10-basis-point assumption is a scenario, not a sourced execution-cost quote. No other fees, market impact or taxes are modeled. ETF operating expenses are reflected in adjusted prices.

Adjustable controls: net modeled target, reserve floor, cost, concentration limit, estimation window and VT mean-return shock. No input is silently relaxed to manufacture feasibility. Original limits and data remain preserved in the saved results.

Monitoring owner: fictional fund committee/portfolio analyst. Review monthly and before annual rebalancing. Pause adoption on source/timing failure, reserve failure, infeasibility or more than ten percentage points of allocation sensitivity. The existing window comparison already triggers review. A price-loss policy must be agreed before live use.
